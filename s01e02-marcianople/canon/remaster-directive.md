## 1. Mission

Produce a complete first-draft illustrated episode about the Gothic crossing into the Roman Empire, the degradation that follows, Fritigern's counteroperation at Marcianople, the Roman defeat, and the mercy that defines the inheritance passed to young Alaric.

This is not a slideshow and not an attempt to simulate a live-action film. It is a narrated, musically structured visual novel using original illustrated stills, restrained animation, designed typography, sound, and TTS narration.

The episode's central statement is:

> Rome mistook hunger for helplessness. The Goths came for land, family, agriculture, and protection. Rome forced weapons back into the hands of a people already trained to use them.

The emotional center is always family. Strategy, battle, and historical consequence emerge from the destruction and defense of families—not from a fascination with conquest.

---

## 2. Historical and Fictional Contract

The production is a clearly labeled fictionalized retelling built around historical events.

### Historical anchors

- Gothic refugees sought admission across the Danube in 376 CE.
- Roman authorities exploited, starved, humiliated, and failed to provision them.
- Ancient accounts describe desperate Goths trading children into slavery for food.
- Gothic leaders attended a banquet hosted by Lupicinus at Marcianople.
- Fighting erupted; Fritigern escaped and rejoined his people.
- Lupicinus marched against the Goths approximately nine miles from Marcianople.
- The Roman force was decisively defeated; Lupicinus survived and escaped.
- Many Goths recovered or acquired Roman weapons from defeated soldiers.

### Deliberate fictional inventions

- Fritigern is Alaric's father.
- Young Alaric and his older brother are present during the migration and Marcianople crisis.
- Alaric's older brother is exchanged for a loaf of bread.
- Fritigern knows the banquet is an execution trap before accepting.
- The banquet retinue conducts a coordinated counteroperation: guard killings, uniform exchange, armory infiltration, substitution of rocks, and wagon extraction of the Goths' confiscated weapons.
- A limited cache of personal weapons was smuggled through careless Roman searches and enables the counteroperation.
- Fritigern defeats Lupicinus near the family wagons, sees Alaric watching, and chooses mercy.
- Alaric later carries a damaged Roman helmet from the battlefield as foreshadowing of his Roman military service.

Do not silently present invented events as established fact. Use an opening or closing card such as:

> Inspired by the Gothic migration and war of 376–382 CE. Some characters, relationships, chronology, and events have been dramatized.

## 3. Episode Architecture

The episode has three primary movements and a short denouement.

### Movement I — Crossing / Mistreatment

**Emotional motion:** hope → uncertainty → denial → degradation → irreversible family loss.

The Goths cross voluntarily after surrendering weapons in exchange for protection and land. Rome's promise closes behind them. Food becomes leverage. The treatment becomes openly inhuman. Alaric's older brother is taken in exchange for bread. The banquet invitation appears to offer reconciliation.

### Movement II — Banquet / Heist

**Emotional motion:** Roman control → exposed trap → violent reversal → concealed success under pressure.

The audience sees Roman soldiers preparing blades and initially believes it possesses dramatic irony: Lupicinus has invited Fritigern into a murder trap. At the banquet, Fritigern takes Lupicinus hostage. The retinue kills guards, assumes Roman clothing, enters the armory, retrieves the Goths' own confiscated weapons, replaces loads with rocks, and moves the wagons through a rear gate. The plot twist reveals that Fritigern knew the Roman plan before entering.

### Movement III — Battle / Mercy

**Emotional motion:** desperate preparation → Roman contempt → Gothic transformation → terror → reversal → mercy.

Weapons reach a wooded clearing before the Roman force. Children, wounded people, and elders are concealed in low migration wagons. Able-bodied Goths cast off travel cloaks and reveal practiced martial competence. The Romans expect to slaughter unarmed refugees and encounter trained, hungry people fighting for their families. Roman confidence collapses into surprise and fear. Fritigern defeats Lupicinus but lowers his weapon when he sees Alaric watching.

### Denouement — Inheritance

The mercy image fades into a quiet battlefield. Alaric stands alone with downward gaze, carrying an oversized damaged Roman officer's helmet with a distinctive broken transverse plume. Broken geometric elements divide the horizon. The image foreshadows the child's future inside—and eventually against—the Roman military system.

Cut to black.

---

## 4. Core Dramatic Reversals

1. The river appears to be deliverance; it becomes containment.
2. The banquet appears to be reconciliation; it is an execution trap.
3. The Goths appear trapped by the banquet; Fritigern entered with a plan.
4. The refugees appear militarily helpless; Rome has mistaken deprivation for incapacity.
5. Victory appears to end in vengeance; Fritigern chooses mercy for his son's sake.
6. The helmet appears to be battlefield debris; it becomes Alaric's inheritance.

Every scene must serve at least one reversal or deepen the family stakes. Attractive images that do neither should not enter the cut.

## 5. Visual Languages

### A. Fractured-world illustration — primary reality

Use for the crossing, denial, deprivation, family separation, battle preparation, open battle, mercy, and aftermath.

**Properties:**

- Refined hand-drawn digital illustration.
- Natural, readable anatomy and expressive faces.
- Clean ink contours with lightly textured color.
- Pale Thracian blue, dirty ivory, charcoal, muted earth, restrained ember-red.
- Geometric paper fractures, displaced horizon segments, and white voids.
- Abstraction remains primarily in sky, ground, architecture, and composition—not across essential faces or gestures.

The degree of fracture increases with broken promises:

| Stage | Fracture level |
| --- | --- |
| crossing | almost intact |
| denial | hairline cracks |
| humiliation | separated planes |
| brother transaction | composition torn apart |
| battle | shattered but still legible |
| mercy | fractures admit light |
| final helmet | vast negative space and unresolved breaks |

### B. Red-black brutalist screen-print — counteroperation

Use for the Roman trap preparation, banquet violence, guard killings, uniform exchange, and armory infiltration.

**Properties:**

- Soot black, dirty bone, iron gray, violent vermilion.
- Distressed screen-print / risograph grain.
- Flat compressed space and monumental silhouettes.
- Exaggerated angular anatomy without losing character identity.
- Red begins as controlled Roman authority—cloaks, wine, blade reflections—and floods the frame when control reverses.

### C. Indigo heist exterior — escape

Use once the weapon wagons clear the postern gate.

**Properties:**

- Midnight indigo, blue-black, cold bone, low tarnished amber.
- Cloaked riders and wagon silhouettes.
- Hidden weapons, low light, speed without celebration.
- Same brutalist construction as the banquet, cooled into secrecy.

### D. Nightmare memory — Alaric's trauma

Use sparingly for the brothers being separated and later Season Two memory recurrences.

**Properties:**

- Expressionist xerox-and-crayon cut-paper animation.
- Radical scale distortion and jointed marionette bodies.
- Soldiers' arms become impossibly long; laughing mouths become empty crescents.
- The brother's rust-red scarf stretches like an umbilical cord across the frame.
- The loaf becomes a dead moon; the Danube rises vertically filled with surrendered swords.
- Terrifying but non-gory. Never cute, anime, polished fantasy, or generic painterly imagery.

### E. Cover and bookend language

- **Season cover:** constructivist mixed-media portrait of Fritigern; extreme crop, teal/ember/bone, torn rectangles, scraped ink, direct gaze.
- **Roman back cover/inset:** tactile, nearly realistic empty armor husk, ruined shield, helmet on ground, gray-sepia battlefield.
- The living Gothic face and empty Roman shell are conceptual bookends.

## 7. Vehicle and Material Accuracy

Never generate nineteenth-century Conestoga or prairie wagons.

Gothic migration vehicles are:

- low, squat, four-wheeled timber carts;
- rectangular plank beds with short sideboards;
- thick wooden wheels, optionally bound with narrow iron tires;
- simple pole axles and visible joinery;
- irregular dark felt, wool, or rawhide coverings tied over low shallow hoops;
- patched brown, gray, and charcoal covers sagging close above cargo;
- pulled by oxen, mules, or small horses.

**Explicit negatives:** no Conestoga wagon, prairie wagon, tall white canvas bonnet, stagecoach, ornate carriage.

## 9. Prompt Engineering Rules

All image prompts must obey:

- Target **750–950 characters**; hard maximum **1,000** unless explicitly overridden.
- Describe only visible image content. Do not narrate plot, motivation, or future events.
- Fully describe every visible principal character in every prompt.
- Specify composition before style.
- Specify period equipment and vehicles concretely.
- Use character names only as metadata; never rely on a name to communicate appearance.
- End with visual medium, palette, abstraction level, and exclusions.
- Keep abstraction off essential faces and gestures unless the shot is explicitly a nightmare.
- Reject generic live-action phrases: cinematic still, movie lighting, TV frame, actor portrait.
- Reject generic animation phrases: anime, Ghibli, glossy concept art, painterly fantasy.

Suggested prompt data structure:

```json
{
  "shot_id": "B06",
  "scene": "battle",
  "purpose": "Roman first realization",
  "prompt": "...",
  "negative": ["photorealism", "movie still", "fantasy armor", "text"],
  "aspect_ratio": "16:9",
  "stylize": 200,
  "chaos": 2,
  "character_count": 1,
  "status": "draft"
}
```

Build a prompt validator that fails if `prompt.length > 1000` and warns when a named principal appears without at least three physical anchors.

## 11. Narration and Dialogue

**Narrator:** Restrained, intimate historical voice. Never describe what the image already shows. Pronounce: Fritigern, Lupicinus, Marcianople, Alaric.

**Fritigern's battle address** (working dialogue):

> Children beneath the wagons. The wounded among the trees.  
> We crossed the river for land—not war. For bread. For peace.  
> But they come believing hunger has made us helpless.  
> If you can string a bow, string it.  
> If you can raise a shield, stand beside me.  
> If you can lift a sword—  
> let it fall upon the men who have come to murder your family.

**Mercy subtext** (optional narration if unclear):

> He had taken up the knife for his child. He would not teach the child to worship it.

## 21. Non-Negotiable Creative Principles

1. Family before empire.
2. The Goths seek peace; violence is forced upon them.
3. They are culturally martial, not magically transformed farmers.
4. Roman cruelty is institutional and human—not supernatural.
5. The heist demonstrates intelligence, organization, and foresight.
6. Mercy is the victory that matters.
7. Alaric's future is foreshadowed, never explained aloud.
8. Illustration remains illustration. Never drift toward movie stills.
9. Prompts describe images; narration tells meaning.
10. Continuity outranks isolated beauty.
